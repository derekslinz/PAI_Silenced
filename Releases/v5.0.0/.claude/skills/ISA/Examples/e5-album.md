Problem

The artist has months of accumulated sketches, voice memos, and half-finished arrangements scattered across a project folder, three notebooks, and an old field-recorder full of ocean and harbor sounds. None of it is a record. The work has stalled at the place every long-form creative project stalls: the gap between "lots of material" and "a thing the world can listen to from start to finish." The pattern across the artist's previous attempts has been the same — composition opens too many doors, mixing extends into tweaking forever, mastering gets handed to someone else and the result sounds like everyone else's record. This time the goal is to take tracks from blank page to mastered release within a -month window without that pattern repeating.

Vision

A -track instrumental album titled `Mariner Frequencies` — roughly minutes of patient, harbor-adjacent ambient-electronic music threaded with field recordings made along the coast at dawn. Listeners encounter it on a streaming platform or a Bandcamp page, press play, and stop scrolling before track three. They listen all the way through, then go back to track and listen to it three more times in a row. They text a friend a link with the words "play this with headphones." That is the euphoric surprise: the record holds attention for minutes the first time and pulls the listener back to a specific track within the same listening session.

Out of Scope

- No vocals.Instrumental record only. No lyric sheet, no vocalist features, no spoken word.
- No live-band recording.Solo project — synthesizers, programmed drums, processed guitars, field recordings. No drum kit miking, no string sections, no ensemble.
- No physical media in v.Streaming + Bandcamp digital release first. Vinyl is a vconversation if the album lands.
- No music videos.A single static cover image and twelve track titles is the entire visual surface for release.
- No tour.This is a recorded-music project. Live performance is a separate decision after release.
- No major-label submission.Self-released through Bandcamp + DistroKid to streaming. No A&R outreach.
- No collaborations on this record.Outside engineers (mixing, mastering) are paid services, not creative co-authors. No "featuring" credits.

Principles

- A finished mediocre record beats an unfinished masterpiece. The album exists to be heard, not to be perfect.
- Constraints are the friend of taste. A -month window with a fixed track count forces decisions; an open-ended schedule has historically produced no album at all.
- Field recordings are first-class compositional material, not decoration. The harbor at dawn is the second instrument on this record.
- Patience over density. The reference points are records that breathe. If a section feels like it needs another layer, it probably needs less.
- The mix is for headphones first, car speakers second, laptop speakers third. The record is being made for people who put on headphones intentionally.
- Mastering is a craft this artist does not have. It gets paid for and outsourced to someone whose ear is calibrated for streaming-platform delivery.
