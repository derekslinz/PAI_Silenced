Phases and Events — Unified Structural Map

The positional reference for story structure. This document maps WHERE every mandatory event falls in a story, organized by three acts and their internal phases. It synthesizes Syd Field, Blake Snyder (Save the Cat), Christopher Vogler (Hero's Journey), Steven Pressfield, Robert McKee, and Dan Harmon into a single unified timeline.

For the PHILOSOPHY behind these positions (why each framework works), see `StoryStructures.md`. For Pressfield's storytelling philosophy specifically, see `PressfieldFramework.md`. For character psychology, see `StorrFramework.md`.

---

The Three Acts

| Act | Range | Purpose | Pressfield's Clothesline |
|-----|-------|---------|--------------------------|
| Act I: Setup| -% | Establish world, character, flaw; introduce concept and conflict | String the clothesline — hook + concept + stakes |
| Act II-A: Ascending Action| -% | Hero engages with the new world; escalating complications | Hang the major items — complications and escalation |
| Act II-B: Descending Action| -% | Forces close in; hero's flaw exposed; mounting pressure | The clothesline bows under weight — crisis builds |
| Act III: Resolution| -% | Transformation tested; climax; new equilibrium | Pull taut and snap — climax resolves the concept |

---

Act I: Setup (-%)

Phase : Opening Image / Ordinary World (-%)

What happens:The "before" snapshot. The hero in their normal environment, operating within their sacred flaw. The world appears stable.

Mandatory events:- Opening Image(-%): A single visual or scene that encapsulates the hero's current state. This will be mirrored by the Final Image to show transformation.
- Character Introduction: The protagonist's behavior demonstrates (not states) their sacred flaw.

Layer priorities:World, Character Change, Prose (establish voice/tone)

Phase : Theme and Setup (-%)

What happens:The world is fully established. Key relationships are introduced. Someone (not the protagonist) hints at the theme.

Mandatory events:- Theme Stated(~%): A secondary character articulates the theme-question, often casually. The protagonist doesn't recognize its importance. (Pressfield: the theme-question planted early.)
- Supporting Cast Introduction: Key allies, mentors, and the antagonist's presence (directly or by shadow).

Layer priorities:Meaning, Relationships, World

Phase : Catalyst / Inciting Incident (-%)

What happens:An event disrupts the ordinary world. This is not the hero's choice — it happens TO them. It directly targets the sacred flaw.

Mandatory events:- Inciting Incident / Catalyst(~%): The event that upsets the balance. Must be specific, concrete, and impossible to ignore. (Story Grid: causal or coincidental inciting incident.)

Layer priorities:Plot, Character Change, Mystery (first major question opens)

Phase : Debate / Refusal (-%)

What happens:The hero resists the call. This is the sacred flaw's defense mechanism in action — the hero tries to maintain their flawed model of the world.

Mandatory events:- Debate Sequence(-%): The hero weighs options, seeks alternatives, tries to avoid commitment. This is NOT padding — it demonstrates the strength of the flaw's grip.

Layer priorities:Character Change, Relationships

Phase : Break Into Two / First Plot Point (-%)

What happens:The hero commits to the new world/approach. This is a decision, not an event — the hero CHOOSES to cross the threshold. The old world is left behind.

Mandatory events:- First Plot Point / Break Into Two(~%): An active choice by the protagonist to engage with the conflict. The act boundary. (Vogler: Crossing the Threshold. Field: Plot Point I.)

Layer priorities:Plot, Character Change

---

Act II-A: Ascending Action (-%)

Phase : B-Story and New World (-%)

What happens:New characters enter. The B-Story (usually a relationship that will teach the theme) begins. The hero explores the unfamiliar world.

Mandatory events:- B-Story Introduction(~-%): A new relationship begins — often the love interest, mentor, or unexpected ally. This relationship will eventually deliver the thematic lesson.

Layer priorities:Relationships, Meaning, World

Phase : Fun and Games / Tests and Allies (-%)

What happens:The promise of the premise is delivered. The hero applies their skills to the new world. Early victories (or entertaining failures). The audience gets what they came for.

Mandatory events:- Promise of the Premise: Multiple scenes delivering on the concept's promise. If the concept is "dinosaur theme park," this is where we see dinosaurs.
- First Pinch Point(~%): A reminder of the antagonist's power and threat. The villain (or opposition force) flexes, demonstrating what's at stake. (Pressfield: the villain IS the story.)

Layer priorities:Plot, World, Mystery (secondary mysteries develop)

Phase : Midpoint / Point of No Return (%)

What happens:The structural pivot. A major revelation or reversal that transforms the nature of the conflict. The hero can no longer go back.

Mandatory events:- Midpoint(%): Either a false victory (things seem great but aren't) or a false defeat (things seem terrible but contain a seed of hope). The stakes escalate permanently. (Harmon: "Find what they wanted." McKee: "Midpoint reversal.")

What changes at the midpoint:- The hero shifts from reactive to proactive (or vice versa)
- New information reframes everything
- The personal and public stakes merge
- The ticking clock activates or accelerates

Layer priorities:Plot, Mystery (major reveal or redirect), Character Change

---

Act II-B: Descending Action (-%)

Phase : Bad Guys Close In (-%)

What happens:Internal and external forces tighten. The team fractures. Allies waver. The villain gains ground. The hero's flaw creates escalating problems.

Mandatory events:- Escalating Complications(-%): A series of progressive complications where each attempt to solve the problem makes things worse. The hero's flawed approach is increasingly exposed.
- Second Pinch Point(~%): Another reminder of the antagonist's power, more visceral than the first. The villain's full capability is revealed.

Layer priorities:Character Change (flaw under pressure), Relationships (bonds tested), Plot

Phase : All Is Lost (%)

What happens:The nadir. Everything the hero has tried using their flawed worldview has failed. A "whiff of death" — something or someone connected to the hero dies or is destroyed (literally or metaphorically).

Mandatory events:- All Is Lost(~%): The lowest point. The old model shatters. (Pressfield: every flaw-based strategy has failed. Storr: the flawed model must completely break.)

Layer priorities:Character Change (sacred flaw fully exposed), Meaning

Phase : Dark Night of the Soul (-%)

What happens:The emotional aftermath of All Is Lost. Despair, reflection, and finally the decision to try something fundamentally different. This is where the B-Story lesson arrives.

Mandatory events:- Dark Night of the Soul(-%): The hero sits with their failure. In this silence, the B-Story relationship delivers the thematic insight — often without realizing it. The hero sees the truth.
- Second Plot Point / Break Into Three(~%): The hero synthesizes want and need. A new approach emerges — one that requires abandoning the sacred flaw. (Field: Plot Point II.)

Layer priorities:Character Change (moment of truth), Meaning (theme crystallizes), Relationships (B-Story delivers)

---

Act III: Resolution (-%)

Phase : Finale (-%)

What happens:The transformed (or transforming) hero enacts a new plan. The climax tests whether the transformation is genuine.

Mandatory events:- The Five-Point Finale(Snyder's structure):
  . Gathering the Team— The hero assembles resources using new understanding
  . Executing the Plan— The new approach is put into action
  . High Tower Surprise— An unexpected obstacle threatens everything (the villain's last move)
  . Dig Deep Down— The hero must reach into their transformed self for the final push
  . Execution of New Plan— The hero defeats the opposition by embodying the theme's answer

- Climax(~%): The confrontation between hero and villain that answers the theme-question through action. (Pressfield: hero defeats villain by proving the theme's thesis. Storr: the crisis forces the final choice between flaw and need.)

Layer priorities:All seven layers converge — this is the story's culmination

Phase : Final Image / Resolution (-%)

What happens:The "after" snapshot. The new equilibrium. The hero in possession of the Gift.

Mandatory events:- Resolution / Denouement(-%): Loose ends resolved. New status quo established. The world has changed because the hero changed (positive arc) or the hero is destroyed because they refused to change (tragedy).
- Final Image(-%): Mirrors the Opening Image to demonstrate transformation. (Pressfield: the Gift is visible — the hero lives differently.)

Layer priorities:Meaning (final thematic statement through image), Character Change (transformation visible), World (new equilibrium)

Using This Map

During Story Bible Construction (BuildBible)

. Map your story's major events to this timeline
. For each phase, identify what happens across all seven layers (see `StoryLayers.md`)
. Verify every mandatory event is planned
. Check that the Pressfield elements are in place (concept → clothesline → hook → villain → all is lost → gift)

During Chapter Writing (WriteChapter)

. Identify which phase(s) the chapter covers
. Load the mandatory events for that phase
. Check the layer priorities for that phase
. Write with awareness of where you are on the tension curve

Phase Checklist for Story Bible

- [ ] Opening Image planned (mirrors Final Image)
- [ ] Theme-Question stated by secondary character
- [ ] Inciting Incident targets the sacred flaw
- [ ] Debate demonstrates flaw's grip
- [ ] First Plot Point is hero's active CHOICE
- [ ] B-Story relationship carries thematic lesson
- [ ] Fun and Games delivers on concept premise
- [ ] Both Pinch Points show villain's power
- [ ] Midpoint permanently escalates stakes
- [ ] All Is Lost shatters the flawed model
- [ ] Dark Night delivers B-Story insight
- [ ] Climax answers theme-question through action
- [ ] Final Image shows the Gift
